In-Custody Informant



View the statement of an in-custody informant against the defendant with caution and close scrutiny. In evaluating such a statement, you should consider the extent to which it may have been influenced by the receipt of, or expectation of, any benefits. This does not mean that you may arbitrarily disregard such statement, but you should give it the weight to which you find it to be entitled in the light of all the evidence in the case.

You may not convict the defendant of example charged crime s based on the statement of that in-custody informant alone.

You may use the statement of an in-custody informant against the defendant only if:

1. The statement is supported by other evidence that you believe;

2. That supporting evidence is independent of the statement;

AND

3. That supporting evidence connects the defendant to the commission of the crime. The supporting evidence is not sufficient if it merely shows that the charged crime was committed.

This supporting evidence requirement does not apply where the testimony of an in-custody informant is offered for any purpose other than proving guilt.
