Natural and Probable Consequences Doctrine (Target and Non-Target Offenses Charged)



The defendant is charged in Counts example f1 with example target offense and in Countss example f3 with example nontarget offense.

You must first decide whether the defendant is guilty of example target offense. If you find the defendant is guilty of this crime, you must then decide whether they are guilty of example nontarget offense.

Under certain circumstances, a person who is guilty of one crime may also be guilty of other crimes that were committed at the same time.

To prove that the defendant is guilty of example nontarget offense, the People must prove that:

1. The defendant is guilty of example target offense;

2. During the commission of example target offense a coparticipant in that example target offense committed the crime of example nontarget offense;

AND

3. Under all of the circumstances, a reasonable person in the defendant’s position would have known that the commission of example nontarget offense was a natural and probable consequence of the commission of the example target offense.

A coparticipant in a crime is the perpetrator or anyone who aided and abetted the perpetrator. It does not include a victim or innocent bystander.

A natural and probable consequence is one that a reasonable person would know is likely to happen if nothing unusual intervenes. In deciding whether a consequence is natural and probable, consider all of the circumstances established by the evidence.

Do not consider evidence of defendant’s intoxication in deciding whether example nontarget offense was a natural and probable consequence of example target offense.

To decide whether the crime of example nontarget offense was committed, please refer to the separate instructions that I have given you on that crime.

The People allege that the defendant originally intended to aid and abet the commission of either example target offense or example other target offense. The defendant is guilty of example nontarget offense if the People have proved that the defendant aided and abetted either example target offense or example other target offense and that example nontarget offense was the natural and probable consequence of either example target offense or example other target offense. However, you do not need to agree on which of these two crimes the defendant aided and abetted.
