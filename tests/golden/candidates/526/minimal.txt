Implied Malice Murder: Aiding and Abetting



To prove that the defendant is guilty of aiding and abetting murder by acting with implied malice, the People must prove that:

1. The perpetrator committed act that was dangerous to human life;

2. The perpetrator’s act caused the death of another person;

3. The defendant knew that the perpetrator intended to commit the act that was dangerous to human life;

4. Before or during the commission of the perpetrator’s act, the defendant intended to aid and abet the perpetrator in committing the act that was dangerous to human life;

5. Before or during the commission of the perpetrator’s act, the defendant knew the perpetrator’s act was dangerous to human life, and the defendant deliberately acted with conscious disregard for human life;

AND

6. By words or conduct, the defendant did in fact aid and abet the perpetrator’s commission of the act.

If all of these requirements are proved, the defendant does not need to actually have been present when the crime was committed to be guilty as an aider and abettor.

Someone aids and abets a crime if he or she knows of the perpetrator’s unlawful purpose and he or she specifically intends to, and does in fact, aid, facilitate, promote, encourage, or instigate the perpetrator’s commission of that crime.

An act is dangerous to human life if there is a high degree of probability that the act will result in death.
