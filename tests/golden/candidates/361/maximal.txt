Failure to Explain or Deny Adverse Evidence



If the defendant failed in their testimony to explain or deny evidence against them, and if they could reasonably be expected to have done so based on what they knew, you may consider their failure to explain or deny in evaluating that evidence. Any such failure is not enough by itself to prove guilt. The People must still prove the defendant guilty beyond a reasonable doubt.

If the defendant failed to explain or deny, it is up to you to decide the meaning and importance of that failure.
